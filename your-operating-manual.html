Why this exists

You've built a lot. An AI Profile. A Voice Card. A prompt library that does your actual work. Now we're going to compress all of it into one document — and you'll be able to paste it into any AI on earth and have your setup, your voice, your guardrails travel with you.

No starting over when you switch tools. No re-explaining yourself at the top of every new chat. No generic output that sounds nothing like you. This document is the thing you hand to any AI and it immediately knows who it's working with.

It's also the thing you hand to yourself — a record of how you think and what you've built — so three months from now, you can update one section and re-deploy it everywhere instead of starting from scratch.

The template

Fill this in using everything you've built in this course. Each section tells you exactly where to pull from. Don't overthink the polish — rough and honest beats polished and vague every time.

Your Operating Manual# [Your Name]'s AI Operating Manual
_Last updated: [DATE]_

## WHO I AM
[Pull from your AI Profile page (ai-profile.html). 3-5 sentences about who you are
and what you do. Job title is the start — also include what you make, who you serve,
and what "good work" looks like in your world.]

## HOW I THINK / WHAT I NEED
[From your Onboarding Interview answers. ADHD? Visual thinker? Need step-by-step?
Hate small talk? Process out loud before deciding? Be specific. This is the section
where you give AI permission to work the way your brain actually works.]

## MY VOICE
[From your Voice Card (writing-voice.html). 2-3 sentences describing how you write.
Include 1-2 phrases you actually use. List words or tones you want avoided.
Example: "Direct, warm, a little dry. I say 'here's the thing' and 'to be clear.'
Never formal. Never 'utilize.'"]

## MY STANDARD PROMPTS
[Your top 5-10 prompts from your Prompt Library. Paste the actual text of each one
so AI can call them by name. Label them: MORNING_TRIAGE, EMAIL_DRAFT, WEEKLY_REVIEW, etc.]

## THE WORK I DO
[3-5 sentences: your role, your deliverables, who you serve, and the vocabulary that
matters in your field. If you use specific terms of art, list them. AI should be able
to write a document for your job without needing a glossary.]

## MY GUARDRAILS
[From the AI Safety lesson. What NOT to paste in (client data, personal health info,
anything in your RED bucket). When NOT to use AI (high-stakes decisions, grief,
anything requiring human judgment). Topics you handle yourself, full stop.]

## RESPONSE FORMAT I WANT BY DEFAULT
[How long? What tone? Bullets or paragraphs? Should AI ask clarifying questions or
just make a call? Should it summarize at the end? Be as specific as you want —
this is the default you live with every day.]

## WHEN TO USE COUNCILS
[From the Councils track. Which types of decisions deserve a panel of perspectives.
Which topics you've found benefit from being challenged before you commit.
Example: "Any decision with irreversible consequences. Any time I'm excited
about something I came up with myself."]
💡
Keep it under 800 words
The manual travels as a system prompt or custom instruction. Most platforms have limits — and shorter, more precise instructions work better than long ones anyway. Write the minimum that actually captures you.

Deploy it

Same document, four different drop-zones. Pick the AI you use most and start there. You can deploy to the others when it's convenient — nothing expires.

Claude

Claude Project

  1. Create a new Project in Claude
  2. Project settings → Project Knowledge → paste your full manual
  3. Optionally: Project instructions → paste the first 200 words as a quick orientation
  4. All conversations inside that project now run with your manual active

Tip: name the project after what you do, not your name. "Freelance Design Work" is more useful than "My Setup."

ChatGPT

Custom Instructions

  1. Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions
  2. Box 1 ("What would you like ChatGPT to know?") → paste WHO I AM + HOW I THINK + THE WORK I DO
  3. Box 2 ("How would you like ChatGPT to respond?") → paste MY VOICE + RESPONSE FORMAT
  4. Save → applies to every conversation going forward

ChatGPT splits the manual across two boxes — this split is intentional, it maps to how the boxes are used internally.

Gemini

Gems

  1. Go to Gems → Create new Gem
  2. Paste your full manual into the Instructions field
  3. Name it "[Your name]'s Operating Manual" or something role-based
  4. Pin it for quick access from your sidebar

Gems also accept file uploads — you can attach a PDF of your manual so it stays versioned alongside the instructions.

Any other tool

System prompt / API

  1. Look for "system prompt," "custom personality," or "instructions" in settings
  2. Paste your manual directly — the structure works everywhere
  3. If you're using an API: set the manual as the system message before your first user message
  4. Same approach works for Copilot, Perplexity, Mistral, and any open-weight model

If a tool doesn't have a persistent system prompt, paste the manual at the top of your first message each session. It still works.

Test it before you trust it

Run this exercise once. It takes 10 minutes and it tells you immediately whether the manual is doing its job.

🧪
The test prompt

Use this exact prompt in two places: once in a fresh chat with no manual loaded, and once in a chat where your manual is active.

Test PromptHelp me think about whether to take on a new freelance client.
They want [describe the work], my plate currently looks like [describe your situation],
and I'm leaning [your gut instinct]. What am I not thinking about?

Compare the responses. Does the manual-loaded version sound like advice from someone who knows your work? Does it use your vocabulary? Does it skip the disclaimers you don't need? That gap is what the manual is for.

Without your manual

Generic. Considers things like "work-life balance" and "professional growth opportunities." Sounds like it was written for anyone. Probably includes a numbered list of questions you didn't ask for.

With your manual active

Anchored to how you actually work. References your capacity, your voice, your known guardrails. Asks the follow-up that matters for your situation. Skips the filler. Sounds like a smart colleague who knows your context.

When life shifts

🚧
Three scenarios you'll hit eventually

Moving from personal to work account

  • Safe to bring: your prompts, your voice, your frameworks — those are yours, not your employer's
  • Scrub before moving: project memory with personal data, anything about other employers, anything in your RED bucket from the AI Safety lesson
  • Use this as the moment to refresh the whole manual — role changes are the best time to update anyway

Switching AI tools

  • The manual is already the portable artifact — same content, different drop-zone
  • Re-deploy following Section 3 above for whichever platform you're moving to
  • You don't need to rebuild anything. That's exactly why you made this.

Handing off to a colleague or team

  • Share the structure, not your personal content — your voice and WHO I AM sections are yours
  • Point them to Find Your Path so they build their own from scratch
  • A team where everyone has their own manual is more consistent than a team sharing one generic one

Keep it living

The manual is only useful if it reflects who you are right now, not who you were when you wrote it. Here's a simple maintenance rhythm.

After big projects

Did you develop new vocabulary? Shift how you write? Learn a new prompt pattern that replaced an old one? Update MY VOICE and MY STANDARD PROMPTS.

After role changes

WHO I AM and THE WORK I DO need a full rewrite. Don't try to patch — start those sections fresh and port over what still fits.

Quarterly anyway

Even if nothing major changed, read it through once a quarter. You'll spot one or two things that no longer sound like you. 20 minutes, done.

💾
Where to store the master copy
Keep a canonical version somewhere version-controlled: your Notion dashboard (link it from Your Progress), a private GitHub Gist, or a dated document in your notes app. When you update it, date the entry. The goal is: you can always find the current version and you can always see what changed.
