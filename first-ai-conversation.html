Step 1: Open Claude and Say Hello

📊Research

Research on automation trust calibration shows that people who understand a system's limitations use it more effectively (Lee & See, 2004). A 2025 ACM CSCW study of 61 neurodivergent communities identified 20 distinct LLM use cases across 5 themes — emotional well-being, mental health, interpersonal communication, learning, and professional productivity — many of which you'll likely recognize as your own[1].

💡What good looks like

Claude explains how it generates responses. Key insight: AI sounds confident whether right or wrong. The tone tells you nothing about accuracy.

✅Open Claude and try this first
  1. Open claude.ai
  2. Copy and paste this prompt into a new conversation:
Hi! I'm trying AI for the first time. I want to understand what you can and can't do. Can you explain in 3-4 casual sentences: what are you actually doing when I ask you a question? Keep it non-technical.
  1. Read the response. Notice how it sounds confident and clear.
  2. Reflect: Did Claude sound like it really knew? Did it explain itself in a way that made sense?

Step 2: Test the Confidence Thing

✅Copy paste this 
Give me 3 passionate reasons why pineapple belongs on pizza. Then 3 reasons why it doesn't. After both, tell me: were you 'believing' either side?
⚠️The big takeaway

AI argued both sides with equal confidence. Confidence and accuracy are completely separate. This is huge. AI will argue any position with the same tone. You have to be the judge.

Step 3: Have a Real Conversation

✅Think of something you need help with

Fill in the brackets and paste this prompt:

I need help with [YOUR TASK]. Before you help me: what's one thing you'd want to know? Ask me that one question first.

Examples to fill in:

  • "I need help with organizing my files"
  • "I need help with writing a tough email"
  • "I need help with planning a project"
  • "I need help with understanding a topic I'm confused about"
💡What good looks like

The response addresses YOUR situation, not generic advice. Claude asked you a question first instead of lecturing. That's how conversations work. The best AI interactions are back-and-forths, not one-way answers.

Step 4: Save What You Learned

ℹ️Update your profile

In your AI Profile (or notes), jot down:

  • Your current experience level with AI (brand new, some experience, etc.)
  • One thing that surprised you about AI
  • One question you still have
ℹ️AI Quick Reference Card

Keep this handy:

  • AI generates based on patterns. It's trained on tons of text and predicts what words come next. It's not thinking like you are.
  • Confidence ≠ accuracy. AI sounds sure about things it's actually unsure about.
  • Ask questions first. When you ask Claude what it needs to know, you get better answers.
  • You're in charge. AI is a tool. You decide what to use and what to ignore.

Occupational therapists studying ADHD adults have started calling AI a "potent self-management resource" for working memory, flexibility, and organization — placing it alongside coaching and body-doubling in the clinical toolkit[8]. If you ever wonder whether you're "allowed" to lean on it, that's your answer.

Developer Zack Proser has written about Claude as an "external brain" for his autism and ADHD — context externalization, gentle guardrails, reduced shame cycles[15]. You're not alone in finding this transformative.
