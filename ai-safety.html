Phase 4 · Use AI responsibly
⏱ Time & Energy
15 min · any energy · You’ll need: your employer’s AI policy if you have one (look in the employee handbook or Slack #ai channel)
The core problem: Everything you paste into a public AI tool may train a future model, may be stored, may be accessible to the vendor’s employees. For most uses that’s fine. For some — PII, proprietary work, credentials — it’s career-ending.

The three buckets

🔬 Why this works
Bucketing reduces decision fatigue. Instead of weighing every paste, you sort the kind of data once, then apply the matching rule on autopilot. Cognitive offload — especially helpful for ADHD brains where every micro-decision drains the same budget.
☁️ Low energy version
Memorize one rule: “If it would be bad on a billboard, don’t paste it into AI.” Not perfect, but covers 90% of mistakes.
🟢 Green bucket — freely usable
Things that are already public, generic, or fully synthetic. Paste these without thinking twice.
  • Public blog posts, articles, marketing copy
  • Generic templates and how-to questions
  • Open-source code with permissive licenses
  • Made-up examples and synthetic data
  • Your own personal notes you’d be fine sharing publicly
🟡 Yellow bucket — only in approved tools
Internal work data that’s sensitive-but-not-secret. Use enterprise AI (Smartsheet’s sanctioned tools, ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude for Work) where data isn’t used for training. Never paste into a personal account.
  • Internal documents, draft proposals, project plans
  • Anonymized customer data (names removed)
  • Code from your employer’s codebase
  • Meeting notes, roadmaps, OKRs
  • Email drafts referencing internal projects
🔴 Red bucket — never, in any AI tool
If you put any of these into any AI tool, treat it as leaked. Even in “enterprise” tiers, the operational risk isn’t worth it.
  • Credentials — API keys, passwords, tokens, .env files
  • PII for people who didn’t consent — customer names + emails, employee data, health info
  • Regulated data — PHI (HIPAA), financial account numbers, government IDs
  • Unreleased product info — specs, designs, financials before public announcement
  • Legal / HR-sensitive content — investigations, contracts under negotiation, severance terms
  • Anything your employer has explicitly told you not to

The Smartsheet-specific calibration

This will be different for you depending on your employer’s policy. For a tech company like Smartsheet with mature AI usage:

📘 Likely OK (always verify with your team)
  • Using Smartsheet’s sanctioned AI tools for any internal work
  • Pasting public documentation, generic code patterns into personal AI
  • Asking AI for help with general engineering concepts
  • Drafting status updates, refactoring style, brainstorming
⚠️ Likely not OK
  • Pasting actual customer Sheets data, even anonymized, into a personal AI
  • Sharing pre-release feature specs in a public AI chat
  • Putting Smartsheet codebase snippets in a personal Claude / ChatGPT account
  • Asking AI to summarize a confidential meeting recording
⭐ The decisive question
“If this conversation leaked tomorrow — in a vendor breach, a screenshot, a court subpoena — would I be in trouble?” If yes, use the enterprise tool or don’t use AI for this one.

Build your personal AI policy

The cleanest way to never have to decide in the moment is to decide once, in advance. Spend 10 minutes on this. Save it somewhere you’ll see it.

✏️ My AI policy — template
Personal AI policy# My AI Policy — [your name], [date]

## Where I use AI
- Personal stuff → [Claude / ChatGPT / whichever]
- Work stuff → ONLY [enterprise tool name your employer approves]
- I never use personal AI accounts for work data.

## What I never paste anywhere
- Credentials (keys, passwords, tokens, .env contents)
- Customer / coworker names paired with sensitive context
- PHI / financial account numbers / government IDs
- Pre-release product info or specs
- Anything from a confidential channel

## My check before pasting
1. Which bucket: green / yellow / red?
2. Right tool for that bucket?
3. Would a leak embarrass me or my employer?

## What I do when I'm not sure
- Skip the paste. Describe the situation generically instead.
- Or: ask my manager / security team before pasting.

## When I update this
- New job
- New AI tool I start using
- Whenever my employer's AI policy changes
Save this to your Reference library or pin it in Notion. Re-read it once a quarter or when something changes.

AI vs. human: when to choose which

Even setting safety aside, AI is the wrong tool for some jobs. A quick map:

📘 AI vs. human decision matrix
TaskDefault toolWhy
Drafting, brainstorming, refactoring styleAISpeed, no judgment risk
Learning a new technical topicAI + docsAI for shape, docs for ground truth
Translation / summarizationAIVolume use case, low stakes
Emotional support, hard feedbackHumanAI fakes warmth. People know the difference.
Career decisions, big life choicesHuman (mentor, therapist)You need someone who knows you
Anything involving real people’s sensitive infoHuman / approved toolSafety bucket above
Anything where being wrong is dangerous (medical, legal, financial)Professional + AI for prepUse AI to ask better questions, not for answers
Quick factual lookupSearch engine firstFaster, citable, no hallucination
⭐ The ND-specific note
Neurodivergent brains often love AI for things humans are bad at (judgment-free brainstorming, repeated questions, drafting hard emails). That’s real. But AI also can’t notice when you’re burnt out, can’t advocate for you, can’t reality-check whether your boss is actually being unreasonable. Keep humans in the loop for the human stuff.
