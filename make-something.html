LESSON 12

Make Something: Creative Work with AI

AI is your collaborator, not your author. Here's how to finish real creative work.

← MasterClass Home
Phase 4 of 5 — Go Deeper
Prereqs: Something you want to make. Time: 20–40 min. Energy: Any. Pick: One medium, one small thing to finish.
AI is a collaborator, not the author. You bring the taste, the intent, and the final call. AI brings speed, options, and a tireless first draft. The work is still yours — that's the whole point.
☁️
Low energy version
Pick ONE thing you'd like to exist by tonight. Write a two-sentence brief. Hand it to AI. Edit what comes back once. Ship it. That counts as making something.

The one move: write the brief, then direct

Most creative misfires with AI come from asking it to decide instead of asking it to execute a decision you made. The fix is a brief. You set the constraints — audience, tone, format, the one thing it must do — and AI fills in the labor. Then you edit like an editor, not a spectator.

✏️
Brief → artifact — try this
Drop this into Claude: 
Creative briefI want to make [WHAT — e.g., "a 6-post LinkedIn series on burnout for neurodivergent founders"].
Audience: [WHO]
Tone: [3 adjectives]
Must do: [the one job this has to accomplish]
Constraints: [length, format, what to avoid]

First, write the brief back to me in 4 lines so I can confirm.
Then produce a first draft of just the first piece.
⭐
What good looks like
The brief it reads back is sharper than what you typed. The first draft is clearly a draft — not polished slop — and gives you something to react to. You immediately know what you'd change. That reaction is the creative work.

Pick the right tool for the medium

One rule carries this whole lesson: Claude writes the brief, the image (or audio, or video) tool makes the artifact. Text models are your director and editor across every medium; specialist tools do the rendering.

✍️ Text — your primary AI
📘
Use Claude or ChatGPT
Essays, posts, scripts, lyrics, outlines, naming, story structure. This is where AI is strongest and where your voice matters most. Feed it 3–5 samples of your writing so the draft sounds like you, not the model.
🎨 Images — GPT Image · Midjourney · Ideogram
📘
Claude writes the brief, the image tool makes the image
Claude can't generate images — it's a writing tool. Use it to write a vivid, specific image prompt (subject, style, lighting, composition, mood), then paste that into GPT Image (in ChatGPT), Midjourney (most aesthetic), or Ideogram (best at text-in-image). Iterate the prompt, not your patience.
🎵 Audio — Suno · ElevenLabs
📘
Music and voice
Suno for songs and instrumental beds from a text prompt; ElevenLabs for narration and voiceover. Write the lyrics or script in Claude first, then generate.
🎬 Video — Runway
📘
Short clips and motion
Runway for short generative clips and image-to-video. Storyboard the shots in text first; video generation is expensive per second, so direct before you generate.

Iterate like an editor

The first output is raw material, not a verdict. Editors don't re-roll endlessly — they make a specific note and ask for a specific change.

✏️
The editor's note — try this
IterateKeep the structure. Change three things:
1. Cut the intro — start on the second paragraph.
2. Make the tone warmer, less corporate.
3. The third point is weak — replace it with a concrete example.
Show me just the revised version.
⭐
What good looks like
You give 2–4 specific notes, not "make it better." Each pass moves toward something you can name. You stop when it's good enough to use — not when it's theoretically perfect.
🧠
ND safeguard: finish one thing
The trap isn't generating — it's generating forever. AI makes new options so cheaply that "one more version" can eat the whole session and you ship nothing. Pick the medium, pick the one piece, set a stopping rule before you start ("two iterations, then I post it"). A finished okay thing beats nine unfinished brilliant ones.
⚖️
Ownership & originality — worth knowing
  • Originality: the more of you in the brief and the edit, the more it's genuinely your work. A two-word prompt yields generic output; your taste and revisions are what make it yours.
  • Rights & training: image/audio/video tools are trained on large datasets and rights are still contested. Don't claim AI output as hand-made, don't imitate a living artist's signature style, and check a tool's commercial-use terms before you sell or publish.
  • Disclosure: when it matters to your audience, say what was AI-assisted. Honesty ages better than the work.
🔬 The evidence, honestly

The executive-function barriers these workflows target — blank-page paralysis, transition cost, perfectionism stall — are well documented. Whether "AI reliably helps you finish" is reasonable, but not settled science. Try it on one real project, keep the parts that get you to "done," and drop anything that adds friction.
